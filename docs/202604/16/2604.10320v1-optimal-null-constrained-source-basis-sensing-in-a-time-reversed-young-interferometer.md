## 摘要
我们开发了一套关于时间反演杨氏（TRY）干涉仪中零值约束参数估计的通用理论，其中测量是通过固定探测器下的可编程源基编码实现的。我们探讨了一个基本问题：如何设计源图案，使其在强制执行真实计量零值（即在工作点处标称响应消失）的同时，保持对参数的有限一阶灵敏度。在通用的散粒噪声受限信道模型下，我们证明了最优零值约束接收器是通过在逆噪声度量下将导数响应投影到与标称背景正交的子空间中获得的。这产生了一个构造性解，其中最优源基编码由逆噪声加权的导数响应给出，并去除了其背景平行分量。我们进一步推导出了一个精确且普适的信息保留律：局部可获取的 Fisher 信息减少了 $1-χ^2$ 倍，其中 $χ$ 量化了标称响应向量与导数响应向量之间的逆噪声重叠。该结果为执行零值约束的代价建立了精确的几何解释。数值示例表明，零值编码的 TRY 接收器可以保留几乎全部的局部信息，并且可以使用二进制和仅正值的源图案精确实现。这些发现将源基零值工程确定为一种独特且实际可行的导数模式传感方案，对超分辨率计量和可编程光学测量架构具有重要意义。

## Abstract
We develop a general theory of null-constrained parameter estimation in a time-reversed Young (TRY) interferometer, where measurement is performed through programmable source-basis encoding with a fixed detector. We address the fundamental question of how to design source patterns that enforce a true metrological null -- vanishing nominal response at the operating point -- while preserving finite first-order sensitivity to the parameter. Under a general shot-noise-limited channel model, we show that the optimal null-constrained receiver is obtained by projecting the derivative response onto the subspace orthogonal to the nominal background in the inverse-noise metric. This yields a constructive solution in which the optimal source-basis code is given by the inverse-noise-weighted derivative response with its background-parallel component removed. We further derive an exact and universal information-retention law: the locally accessible Fisher information is reduced by a factor $1-χ^2$, where $χ$ quantifies the inverse-noise overlap between the nominal and derivative response vectors. This result establishes a precise geometric interpretation of the cost of null enforcement. Numerical examples demonstrate the null-coded TRY receivers can retain nearly the full local information and can be accurately implemented using binary and positive-only source patterns. These findings identify source-basis null engineering as a distinct and practically viable modality for derivative-mode sensing, with implications for superresolution metrology and programmable optical measurement architectures.
