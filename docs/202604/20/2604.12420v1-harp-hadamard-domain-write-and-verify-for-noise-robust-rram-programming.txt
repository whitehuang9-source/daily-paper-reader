# HARP: Hadamard-Domain Write-and-Verify for Noise-Robust RRAM Programming 

# Ilhuan Choi, Jiwon Yoo, Yoona Lee, Yewon Jeong, Jason Jaesung Lee, and Woo-Seok Choi 

Dept. of Electrical and Computer Engineering, ISRC, Seoul National University Seoul, South Korea {idisian,wooseokchoi}@snu.ac.kr 

## Abstract 

Write-and-verify (WV) is essential for programming multi-level RRAM weights, yet under scaled-voltage and low-SNR conditions the verify read increasingly limits mapping accuracy, convergence speed and energy. We propose a Hadamard-domain WV framework that improves verify reliability without adding analog hardware. 

HD-PV (Hadamard-Encoded Parallel-Verify) replaces conventional one-hot verify reads with 𝑁 orthogonal Hadamard patterns for an 𝑁 -cell column. Changing the read basis without increasing the column-level read count, inverse Hadamard decoding reduces un-correlated read-noise variance by a factor of 𝑁 and cancels common-mode disturbances. HARP (Hadamard-based ADC-Energy-Reduced Parallel-Verify) further exploits the fact that WV needs only ternary update decisions, not full digital codes, and replaces SAR conver-sions with lightweight compare-only operations. Across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and keyword spotting under severe read noise, conven-tional WV loses over 20 % accuracy on CIFAR-10, while HD-PV and HARP limit the loss to 0.6 % and 1 % under the same memory footprint. Compared to conventional multi-read averaging for noise reduction, HD-PV and HARP achieve comparable accuracy with up to 6.1× and 3.5× lower latency and 6.2× and 9.5× better energy efficiency, respectively. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first application of Hadamard-encoded verification to RRAM WV. 

## 1 Introduction 

Compute-in-Memory (CiM) architectures alleviate the data move-ment bottleneck between memory and processing units in von Neu-mann systems and offer significant energy and throughput benefits for edge-AI workloads [ 8, 11 , 20 ]. Among embedded non-volatile memories (eNVMs), resistive random-access memory (RRAM) en-ables multi-level conductance storage, high density, and strong CMOS compatibility, making it well suited for analog CiM (ACiM) accelerators [2, 12, 23, 26]. For edge-AI, RRAM cells are required to be reprogrammed across different DNN models or inference workloads, and limited device endurance imposes a strict upper bound on allowable write cycles [ 6,12 , 16 ]. Furthermore, as DNN models scale, the number of synaptic weights increases dramatically, demanding larger RRAM arrays and more frequent weight-programming operations. Reliable inference therefore depends on fast and accurate conductance updates .Achieving such updates requires an iterative write-and-verify (WV) process. Two factors make WV increasingly difficult as tech-nology scales. First, RRAM programming is nonlinear, asymmetric, and stochastic because of device mismatch and cycle-to-cycle varia-tion [ 2, 7, 27 ]. Second, scaling shrinks sensing margins and increases the influence of thermal and mismatch-induced noise in column analog-to-digital converters (ADCs) [ 15 , 22 , 24 ]. As a result, the bottleneck of WV shifts from the write pulse to the verify read: noisy readbacks trigger incorrect update decisions, wasting itera-tions and degrading final mapping quality. Viewed as a sequential estimation-and-decision problem, each WV iteration estimates the current cell state from a noisy observation and decides whether to pulse up, down, or stop—and under scaled conditions, the quality of that estimate, not the write pulse, limits convergence. RRAM-based ACiM systems have widely employed iterative WV schemes that repeatedly program and verify each cell conductance until it matches the target value [ 4, 6, 10 , 12 , 16 , 23 , 26 –28 ]. While ef-fective, this iterative process inevitably incurs considerable latency and energy penalties, motivating extensive research to make WV more efficient. Prior studies reduce iteration count using MSB-first programming [ 4 , 6 ], probabilistic termination [ 16 ], or coarse-to-fine programming strategies [ 27 , 28 ]. However, most prior works do not fully capture the circuit-level behavior of the ADC, even though it dominates actual hardware latency and energy—up to 87.8 % in [26] and 63.4 % in [15]. Moreover, limited signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) under scaled supply voltages further constrains accu-rate WV, motivating approaches that improve verify-read quality without proportionally increasing ADC cost. This work addresses this directly. We start from a simple but powerful insight: in column-wise verification, recovering the states of an 𝑁 -cell column already requires 𝑁 read patterns. Conventional schemes use 𝑁 one-hot patterns, i.e., an identity read matrix. From an estimation-theoretic perspective, this is a valid but suboptimal choice. Replacing the identity basis with the 𝑁 rows of a Hadamard matrix yields the best linear unbiased estimator for the cell states under i.i.d. read noise: after inverse Hadamard decoding, the vari-ance of the uncorrelated noise component is reduced by a factor of 𝑁 without any increase in column-level read count. Moreover, because all but the first Hadamard row are balanced, common-mode disturbances are completely canceled for 𝑁 − 1 out of 𝑁 cells. Building on this idea, we further note that WV does not always require a full digital code: it mainly needs to know whether a cell conductance should move up, move down, or stop. HARP therefore performs Hadamard-domain compare-only verification and elim-inates full SAR conversion from the WV process, reducing each Hadamard measurement to one or two comparisons instead of 𝑛 

sequential bit decisions. The resulting framework improves programming fidelity at the circuit level and translates directly into system-level benefit. Be-cause all methods are evaluated under identical cell precision, slice count, and array dimensions, the reported gains result in higher inference accuracy at the same memory footprint. Conversely, the same programming margin can be traded for lower memory provi-sioning at a fixed accuracy target.

## 3.3 HD-PV Algorithm 

The overall HD-PV flow is illustrated in Fig. 8. A column is read through 𝑁 Hadamard patterns to obtain ˆy, which is then decoded via inverse Hadamard transform using digital adders. The decoded estimate ˆw is compared with w∗, and SET/RESET/STOP decisions are generated in parallel for all cells. Column-wise writing is then applied as in Fig. 5. Each cell is frozen once its streak counter exceeds 𝐾 , and HD-PV terminates when all cells are frozen or the maximum iteration count is reached. Compared to conventional one-hot (single-cell) verify reads, HD-PV improves the quality of each verification sweep without increasing the number of column reads per sweep. 

## 4 HARP for Reduced WV Energy 4.1 ADC Costs in HD-PV 

HD-PV improves verify reliability, but it still performs a full-resolution SAR conversion for every Hadamard measurement. This is expen-sive: an 𝑛 -bit SAR ADC executes an 𝑛 -step binary search (see Fig. 7) and incurs 𝑛 -cycle latency and substantial switching energy in both the capacitor array and SAR logic. In practical ACiM macros, where WV must be repeated many times across many columns, this per-read cost dominates latency and energy. The key insight behind HARP is that WV is fundamentally a clas-sification problem, not an estimation problem. The verifier needs to assign each cell to one of three categories {SET, RESET, STOP}—it does not need the full digital code. If the Hadamard-domain mea-surement can be compared directly with the Hadamard-domain target, the verifier can make this classification with far less ADC work.

Inverse Hadamard decoding latency and energy are obtained from 28-nm synthesized adders. Unless otherwise stated, all methods use the same array size, cell precision, and column-wise write backend. column-wise single-cell (CW-SC) WV with one-shot comparison . This baseline uses one-hot read patterns with the same compare-only ADC mode available to HARP, thereby isolating the effect of the read basis from the ADC mode. Multi-read averaging uses full SAR conversion since multi-read averaging requires high-resolution digitized values to compute the average. For a verification sweep of an 𝑁 -cell column, CW-SC, HD-PV, and HARP all perform 𝑁 read patterns. Multi-read averaging with

## 5.3 Latency and Energy 

Fig. 12 further highlights the efficiency advantage of the proposed methods over multi-read averaging. While 5-read averaging can re-cover the inference accuracy of CW-SC, HD-PV and HARP achieve substantially better efficiency. Compared with 5-read averaging, HD-PV and HARP are 6.1× and 3.5× faster, respectively, while improving energy efficiency by 6.2× and 9.5×. This improvement stems from the large ADC overhead incurred by multi-read averag-ing, which requires repeated full-resolution ADC conversions for each read operation. Fig. 13 reports per-column WV latency and energy for CIFAR-10 (ResNet-20). For the 32 ×32 array, CW-SC is competitive at very low noise ( ≤0.1 LSB) because it avoids Hadamard decoding, but its latency grows rapidly once noisy verify reads trigger incorrect up-date decisions and extra iterations. Above 0.4 LSB, CW-SC becomes the slowest method. HD-PV and HARP exhibit only modest latency growth (16 % and 17 % for the 32 ×32 array, and 9.7 % and 8.9 % for the 64 ×64 array) because they maintain stable decision quality as noise increases. Energy shows a different ordering. HD-PV incurs the highest per-read cost because every Hadamard measurement uses a full SAR conversion. HARP, by contrast, combines Hadamard denoising with compare-only ADC operation and consistently achieves the lowest energy in the high-noise regime. Under severe noise ( ≥0.5 LSB) HARP uses ≈65 % of HD-PV’s energy for the 32 ×32 array and ≈67 % of CW-SC’s energy for the 64 ×64 array. Across the evaluated configurations, ADC activity accounts for more than 70 % of WV latency and more than 90 % of WV en-ergy. This explains why reducing both misdirected iterations (via Hadamard denoising) and per-read ADC work (via compare-only mode) is crucial.

Optimization target. Prior works [ 4 , 10 , 16 , 25 , 28 ] focus on the write policy, and these methods optimize decision-making given a fixed observation quality. HD-PV and HARP instead optimize the observation itself—the verify read—by changing the measurement basis to the Hadamard basis. The two approaches are complemen-tary: better observations enable better decisions regardless of the pulse scheduling strategy. 

Circuit-level cost modeling. Of the prior methods, only [ 4]specifies the ADC and evaluates at specific array dimensions. Refer-ences [ 10 , 16 , 25 , 28 ] do not model ADC latency or energy, making it difficult to assess the true WV overhead. HD-PV and HARP include explicit circuit-level modeling with practical ADC parameters. 

ADC-aware verification. HARP is the only method that re-designs the ADC operating mode for WV. By recognizing that WV requires classification rather than full digitization, HARP avoids most SAR work—a unique contribution not addressed by any prior method. Unlike prior studies that estimate WV energy solely from iteration counts, our energy and latency results are obtained from circuit-level simulations with practical parameters. All improvements in Table 2 are normalized against the CW-SC WV baseline—already superior to conventional cell-by-cell WV— indicating that the actual gains of HD-PV and HARP over conven-tional WV are even larger than the ratios reported. 

## 6 Conclusion 

This work presented HD-PV and HARP, two circuit-aware WV schemes for RRAM-based ACiM that address the verify-read bottle-neck. The central insight is that conventional one-hot verification is a statistically suboptimal use of the 𝑁 read patterns already required for an 𝑁 -cell column: Hadamard encoding achieves the optimal estimator, reducing uncorrelated noise by 1/𝑁 and cancel-ing common-mode disturbances for 𝑁 − 1 cells, with no additional analog hardware. HARP further recognizes that WV is a classifica-tion problem rather than an estimation problem, and replaces full ADC conversion with lightweight compare-only decision. The pro-posed methods carry two practical implications for RRAM-based ACiM systems. First, under the iso-memory-footprint condition, HD-PV and HARP recover inference accuracy that would other-wise be lost to verify-read noise. Second, at a fixed accuracy target, the improved programming margin opens a path toward reduced bit-slice count or smaller arrays, potentially lowering area cost. The framework is transparent to the DNN model and training proce-dure, complementary to existing pulse-scheduling optimizations, and implementable within existing macro architectures—making it a practical and scalable addition to the RRAM ACiM design toolkit.
